# Parallel Work

1. Parallel work is the practice of advancing multiple tasks or work streams at the same time instead of finishing one before starting the next. Teams adopt it to shorten overall delivery timelines, keep specialists busy on their own tracks, and surface integration problems earlier rather than later. The trade-off is coordination cost: every additional concurrent stream needs synchronisation points, shared understanding of interfaces, and a mechanism to resolve conflicts when changes overlap.

2. The key enabler of effective parallel work is clear interface boundaries. When modules, services, or documents have well-defined contracts, workers can proceed independently and merge results with confidence. When boundaries are fuzzy or unstated, parallel streams collide: two workers edit the same file, incompatible assumptions emerge at integration, and rework erases the time savings that motivated the parallel approach in the first place. Investing in interface definition before splitting work is therefore not overhead — it is the foundation that makes speed possible.

3. Parallel work also demands explicit ownership and communication rituals. Each stream needs a named owner who is accountable for its outcome, and all owners need a lightweight cadence — daily sync, board update, or automated status — to surface blockers early. Without this, parallel streams silently diverge: one team builds against an assumption that another team has already changed, and the discovery happens at merge time when the cost of reconciliation is highest. The goal is not to eliminate surprises but to shrink the gap between when a surprise occurs and when it is shared.
